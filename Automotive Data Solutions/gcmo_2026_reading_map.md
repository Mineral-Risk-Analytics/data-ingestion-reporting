# GCMO 2026 — Reading Map for Coverage Gaps

**Source:** IEA *Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2026*, 372 pp, CC-BY 4.0 (uploaded 2026-07-19). Newer than the CM Data Explorer spreadsheet we extracted for v3. This is the narrative report — mostly headline shares + capacity charts, not the clean country-share tables the Data Explorer had, but it reaches materials the Explorer doesn't (manganese, phosphate, tin, PGMs, and ~16 strategic minor minerals).

Skim done 2026-07-19: front matter + exec summary (1-8), Other key materials (186-196), Strategic minor minerals (198-212), Downstream market trends (58-69). Remaining high-value sections flagged below for a thorough read.

## A. Sections that DIRECTLY fill our gaps — read thoroughly + extract

### "Other key materials" — pp.186-196 ⭐ HIGHEST VALUE
Covers exactly our two biggest holes, with citable China shares (IEA analysis on Benchmark Mineral Intelligence data):

- **Manganese — battery-grade manganese sulphate (pp.187-189).** *China >95% of global supply in 2025.* Only two operating battery-grade Mn sulphate refineries outside China (Japan, Belgium). Project pipeline diversifying: US ~20% of capacity by 2035 (~140 kt Mn), Canada ~10%, Czechia 5%, Botswana. Supply gap narrowed to ~20% deficit by 2035 (was 45%). → **fills Manganese battery_grade** (currently ore-only).
  - Mined manganese 2025 (p.189): South Africa ~40%, Gabon ~25%, Ghana 10%, Australia 8% → cross-check/refine our ore stage (we have USGS).
- **Phosphoric acid — PPA (pp.190-191).** *China 70% of PPA production in 2025, ~75% by 2035* — "little diversification on the horizon." Morocco 5% / US ~5% / Canada / Belgium by 2035 (together >55% of ex-China capacity). LFP = 55% of EV market, 90% of storage in 2025. → **fills Phosphate battery_grade/downstream** (currently ore-only, refined not even mapped).
  - Phosphate rock mining 2025 (p.191): China 45%, Morocco <15%, US ~10%; reserves Morocco 70% → refine our ore stage.
- Also here (non-launch, optional): **Tin** (mining CN 24 / ID 21 / PE 11; refining CN ~50%), **PGMs** (mined Pt SA ~70 / RU 12; Pd RU 45 / SA), **Silver** (by-product, Mexico-led).

Caveat: these are headline single-country shares + *capacity* charts, not full production country-tables. Enough to build a battery_grade benchmark row anchored on the China share; the small non-China tail comes from the charts or a supplementary source.

## B. Deep-read targets — NOT yet read, high value

- **"Mineral market trends" — pp.20-37.** Almost certainly holds the consolidated *refining-concentration-by-material* data and price series. Exec summary previews it: "average share of top refining country rose to 72% in 2025 (ex-REE), up from 70% in 2023"; "top refiners — Indonesia (nickel), China (others) — >¾ of refined-supply growth." → read for cross-material refining shares + our nickel/graphite/REE stages.
- **"Geopolitical developments" — pp.38-57.** Export-control detail: China Oct-2025 controls on cathode materials, cathode precursors, graphite anode, battery equipment; April-2025 seven-heavy-REE controls (expanded Oct-2025, suspended to Nov-2026); DRC cobalt export quota; Zimbabwe lithium / Mozambique graphite restrictions. → feeds the **regulatory + geopolitical pillars / events workbook**, not shares.
- **Individual material outlooks (Part 1):** Copper 124, Lithium 138, **Nickel 147**, Cobalt 155, **Graphite 163**, **REE 174**. The bolded three map to our thin stages — nickel intermediate (matte/MHP) + sulfate, natural-vs-synthetic graphite, magnet-REE refining. Read for per-material midstream splits + 2035 projections.
- **Annex "Key projection results" — pp.346-371.** Likely the country-level data tables behind the charts. Check here first for clean numbers before estimating from charts.

## C. Non-launch / strategic minor minerals — pp.198-212 ⭐ (you asked for these)
IEA's dedicated section on small-market, high-concentration minerals used in high-tech/aerospace/defence. Directly useful if we broaden coverage beyond the battery core.

- **Top-refiner share scatter (p.199)** for ~16 minerals — approx from chart: Gallium ~98%, Germanium ~70%, Indium ~70%, Tellurium ~76%, Tungsten ~78%, Antimony ~70%, Titanium ~68% (metal), Tantalum ~45%, plus base/battery metals for reference.
- **"For gallium, graphite, manganese and rare earths, top refiner China >90% of global supply" (p.211)** — corroborates our manganese/graphite/REE concentration.
- **Risk ranking bar chart (p.210)** ordering ~25 minerals by total risk: gallium, magnet REE, yttrium, graphite, tungsten, germanium, tellurium, cobalt, molybdenum, titanium, antimony, nickel, vanadium… → a ready external benchmark for our risk scores.
- **IEA's own risk methodology (p.209)** — Supply risk (refining concentration top-1, mining concentration top-1, price volatility, export restriction, supply-demand balance) + Alternative supply routes (number of suppliers ≥5%, substitutability, by-product dependence, price transparency) + Strategic importance. Close cousin of our pillars — useful to benchmark/defend our methodology, and note by-product dependence as a dimension we don't have.
- Application matrices (semiconductors/robotics/servers/telecom p.201; aerospace/defence p.204; summary p.208) — which minerals feed which strategic sector; useful for a "strategic importance" / relevance layer.
- IEA notes deeper analysis on these minerals is coming "in the coming months."
